1. Core Genetic Terminology

Gene & Allele

Gene: DNA segment carrying heritable information[cite: 17, 21].
Allele: Alternative version of a gene (e.g., $Y$ yellow vs $y$ green)[cite: 21].

Phenotype vs Genotype

Phenotype: Observable appearance or traits (e.g., tall, purple flowers)[cite: 22].
Genotype: Genetic allele combination ($PP$, $Pp$, or $pp$)[cite: 22].

Homozygous vs Heterozygous

Homozygous: Pair of identical alleles ($RR$ dominant, $rr$ recessive)[cite: 22].
Heterozygous: Pair of different alleles ($Rr$, hybrid)[cite: 22, 23].

Dominant vs Recessive

Dominant: Trait that shows up/masks recessive trait in heterozygous state[cite: 20, 23].
Recessive: Trait masked unless homozygous ($rr$)[cite: 20, 23].

Filial Generations

P Generation: Parental true-breeding stock[cite: 20].
$F_1$ Generation: First filial offspring (hybrids)[cite: 20].
$F_2$ Generation: Offspring from selfing/crossing $F_1$[cite: 20].

Testcross

Crossing an individual of dominant phenotype but unknown genotype ($D\_$) with a known homozygous recessive ($dd$) to determine if it is homozygous or heterozygous[cite: 5].

2. Mendelian Approach & Laws of Inheritance

Law of Segregation (1st Law)

Monohybrid Cross

"The two alleles for a heritable character segregate during gamete formation and end up in different gametes."[cite: 21]

Cellular Link (Meiosis):

Driven by the separation of homologous chromosomes during Anaphase I of Meiosis[cite: 21, 25].

Standard $F_2$ Ratios ($Pp \times Pp$):
• Genotypic Ratio: 1 $PP$ : 2 $Pp$ : 1 $pp$ (1:2:1)[cite: 23]
• Phenotypic Ratio: 3 Purple : 1 White (3:1)[cite: 20, 23]

Law of Independent Assortment (2nd Law)

Dihybrid Cross

"Two or more genes assort independently—each pair of alleles segregates independently of each other pair during gamete formation."[cite: 23]

Cellular Link (Meiosis):

Non-homologous chromosomes line up and align randomly along the metaphase plate in Metaphase I / Anaphase I[cite: 25].

Standard $F_2$ Phenotypic Ratio ($YyRr \times YyRr$):
• 9 Yellow-Round : 3 Yellow-Wrinkled : 3 Green-Round : 1 Green-Wrinkled (9:3:3:1)[cite: 8, 24]

3. Probability Laws & Solving Methods

Rule 1

Multiplication Rule ("AND" Rule)

The probability of two or more independent events occurring together is equal to the product of their individual probabilities[cite: 2, 14].

$P(\text{Event 1 AND Event 2}) = P(1) \times P(2)$[cite: 2]

Example: Probability of getting $gg$ yellow pods from $Gg \times Gg$:
$P(\text{g from } P_1) \times P(\text{g from } P_2) = \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{4}$[cite: 2, 3]

Rule 2

Addition Rule ("OR" Rule)

The probability of any one of two or more mutually exclusive events occurring is the sum of their individual probabilities[cite: 3, 14].

$P(\text{Event 1 OR Event 2}) = P(1) + P(2)$[cite: 3]

Example: Probability of getting heterozygous $Gg$ from $Gg \times Gg$:
$P(G \text{ egg } \cdot g \text{ sperm}) + P(g \text{ egg } \cdot G \text{ sperm}) = \frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{4} = \frac{1}{2}$[cite: 4]

Punnett Square vs. Forked-Line Method

Punnett Square Method

A diagrammatic grid developed by Reginald Punnett to visualize gamete combinations[cite: 6].

  • Best for monohybrid ($2 \times 2$) or simple dihybrid ($4 \times 4$) crosses[cite: 6, 8].
  • Becomes unwieldy for trihybrid ($8 \times 8 = 64$ squares) or multi-factor crosses[cite: 10].

Forked-Line (Branched-Line) Method

Uses independent probability columns for each gene pair, branching outcomes across loci[cite: 9].

  • Anchored to Law of Independent Assortment[cite: 9].
  • Highly efficient for trihybrid/multihybrid crosses (e.g. $AaBbCc \times AaBbCc$)[cite: 10].

4. Extensions of Mendelian Genetics

A. Degrees of Dominance

  • Incomplete Dominance: Heterozygote shows an intermediate phenotype between the two homozygotes (e.g. Curly $H^C H^C$ × Straight $H^S H^S \rightarrow$ Wavy $H^C H^S$; Snapdragon flower color)[cite: 60, 61].
    Monohybrid $F_2$ phenotypic ratio = 1:2:1 (matches genotypic ratio)[cite: 61].
  • Codominance: Both alleles are fully and simultaneously expressed in heterozygotes without blending (e.g. Roan cattle with red & white hair patches $RW$; ABO blood type $I^A I^B$)[cite: 31, 61, 62].
    Monohybrid $F_2$ phenotypic ratio = 1:2:1[cite: 62].

B. Multiple Allelism

A single gene is controlled by more than two alleles in a population (though an individual still possesses only 2)[cite: 29, 31, 35].

Human ABO Blood Groups ($I^A, I^B, i$):

• Type A ($I^A I^A$ or $I^A i$), Type B ($I^B I^B$ or $I^B i$)[cite: 31]
• Type AB ($I^A I^B$ - Codominant), Type O ($ii$ - Recessive)[cite: 31]

Rabbit Fur Coat Hierarchy:

$C$ (Wild type / Brown) > $c^{ch}$ (Chinchilla) > $c^h$ (Himalayan) > $c$ (Albino)[cite: 33]

C. Polygenic (Quantitative) Inheritance

A single character is influenced by multiple independent genes acting additively, creating a continuous spectrum of phenotypes (bell curve)[cite: 38, 39, 41].

Examples: Human skin color ($AaBbCc$ - 3 gene pairs), eye color, height[cite: 38, 39].

Binomial Expansion formula: $(a+b)^{2n}$ where $n$ = number of gene pairs[cite: 41].

For 3 genes ($2n=6$): $F_2$ phenotypic ratio = 1 : 6 : 15 : 20 : 15 : 6 : 1[cite: 41, 42].

D. Sex-Linked Inheritance

Traits controlled by genes located on sex chromosomes ($X$ or $Y$)[cite: 46, 47].

  • X-Linked Recessive: Expressed in all hemizygous males ($X^b Y$); females require two copies ($X^b X^b$)[cite: 46, 47]. Shows crisscross inheritance (carrier mother to son)[cite: 48, 55].
    Examples: Hemophilia, Red-Green Colorblindness, Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy (DMD)[cite: 48].
  • X-Linked Dominant: Affected males pass to all daughters, 0% sons[cite: 49, 50]. Affected females pass to 50% offspring[cite: 47, 49]. (e.g. Incontinentia pigmenti)[cite: 50].
  • Y-Linked (Holandric): Passed directly from father to 100% of sons[cite: 50, 55]. (e.g. Hypertrichosis of ears)[cite: 46, 50].
